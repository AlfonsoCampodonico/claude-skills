# Recipes

## Front matter (his blog)

```yaml
---
title: Lasagna (my way)
slug: lasagna
excerpt: Venezuelan pasticho. Meat sauce, ham, cheese and a lot of béchamel.
servings: 8
prep_time: 45 min
cook_time: 2 h 30 min
topics: pasta, oven
published_at: YYYY-MM-DD
---
```

The excerpt names the dish and what's in it, in fragments.

## Intro: one to three short sentences

What it is, and the one thing that makes or breaks it.

> Four ingredients and pasta water. The whole trick is keeping the eggs creamy
> instead of scrambled, so the sauce goes on off the heat.

A family or origin story only if he gives you one. Otherwise leave
`[ANECDOTE: where this recipe comes from?]` and ask. Never invent a grandmother,
a childhood or a weekend habit.

Add a second short paragraph for advice that applies before you start ("Make the
ragu the day before if you can. It cuts cleaner and tastes better.").

## Ingredients

- `## Ingredients`, then `### For the <part>` groups when there's more than one
  component.
- Metric: g, ml, l, tbsp, tsp, °C. No cups, no Fahrenheit.
- Prep goes after a comma: "1 large onion, finely chopped".
- Brand or shop hints go in Notes, not in the list.

## Steps

- `## Steps`, then `### <Part>` groups in the order you cook them.
- Imperative, one action per numbered step, with a time and a visual cue: "until
  it coats a spoon, about 10 minutes".
- The why, in one short sentence, where it saves a mistake: "Let the bottom catch
  a little before you stir: that's flavour."
- Fixes inline: "If it's sticky, dust in flour. If it's crumbly, wet your hands."

## Notes

Short paragraphs, not bullets: how long it keeps and how to reheat, where to find
an odd ingredient, a substitution, the vegetarian version, and variations other
families make ("Mine doesn't, but none of them are wrong.").
